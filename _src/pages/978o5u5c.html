Formula One · three moments · told in sound

Three championships were settled in a few seconds each: at a chicane in Japan, at the last corner in Brazil, at a hairpin in Abu Dhabi. Here is where each happened, who was there, and how each era's engine sounded as it unfolded.

At Suzuka's chicane, two team-mates settled a title by refusing to yield

Senna came to the Japanese Grand Prix needing to win to keep his title hopes alive. Prost, his team-mate and rival, led from the start and stayed ahead for most of the afternoon.

On lap 47 Senna dived down the inside at the chicane before the pit straight. Prost turned in. The two McLarens locked wheels and slid to a stop together on the escape road. Prost climbed out. Senna, pushed by marshals, rejoined through the escape road, pitted for a new nose, caught and passed Alessandro Nannini's Benetton, and crossed the line first.

The stewards disqualified him for rejoining by cutting the chicane. Nannini was awarded the win, and Prost became world champion. A year later, at the same circuit, the roles were reversed: Senna drove into Prost at the first corner and the 1990 title was his.

At Interlagos, the title changed hands twice in the final corners

If Massa won his home race, Hamilton needed fifth place to be champion. Massa did his part and led from the front. In the closing laps rain began to fall, and most of the field pitted for intermediate tyres. Glock's Toyota stayed out on dry tyres.

With two laps left, Sebastian Vettel passed Hamilton and pushed him down to sixth. When Massa crossed the line as the winner, the Ferrari garage celebrated a championship. But Glock was losing grip in the wet, and on the final lap Hamilton passed him on the climb out of Junção, the last corner before the finish.

He finished fifth and won the title by one point, 98 to 97. At 23 he was, at the time, the youngest champion in the sport's history. Massa took the podium as runner-up, with a composure that is still remembered.

At Yas Marina, a season that arrived level was decided on a single lap

Verstappen and Hamilton came to the final race of 2021 level on points. Hamilton took the lead at the start and built a comfortable margin over the afternoon.

On lap 53 Nicholas Latifi crashed, and the safety car came out. Verstappen pitted for fresh soft tyres; Hamilton stayed out on old ones. Race director Michael Masi allowed only the lapped cars running between the two leaders to unlap themselves, and the race restarted for the final lap.

Verstappen dived inside at Turn 5, held off Hamilton's counter-attack on the long straight that follows, and won his first world championship. Mercedes' protests were rejected. The FIA later reviewed the race, and Masi was removed from his role.

Each era's engine sings in its own key, set by cylinders and revs

The brightest line in each spectrogram is the firing frequency: how many times per second a cylinder fires. In a four-stroke engine each cylinder fires once every two turns of the crankshaft, so the pitch follows directly from the revs and the number of cylinders.
